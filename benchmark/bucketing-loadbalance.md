# Bucketing load balance on time-evolved operators

How evenly the bucketed `binary_kernel!` distributes work across output
buckets, for operators `O(t)` grown by Heisenberg RK4 evolution under several
models, and how long bucketizing `O(t)` takes. Generated by
`benchmark/bucketing/loadbalance.jl` (18 steps, dt=0.15).

Metrics (imbalances: lower = better, `1.00` is perfect):
- **commutator imbalance** = max/mean per-output-bucket work for `[H, O(t)]` (the RK4 kernel call);
- **occupancy imbalance** = max/mean of `O(t)`'s own per-bucket string count;
- **p99** = 99th-percentile bucket work / mean; **empty** = fraction of empty buckets;
- **bucketize** = wall-clock to sort `O(t)` into buckets (one pass; the hash cost differs by family).

## Reading this

- A strategy parallelizes well when its **commutator imbalance** is close to 1:
  every thread gets a similar share of `[H, O(t)]`.
- `lowbits` keys on the low qubits only and is badly imbalanced for spatially-local
  operators; `Folded`/`Mixing` mix all qubits. `Mixing` (the deterministic full-rank
  matrix `default_strategy` uses) balances best but its per-string hash (B popcounts)
  makes **bucketize** more expensive — the balance/cost trade-off to weigh against the
  kernel's `|A|·|B|` work.
- More buckets (higher `b`) give finer load-balancing granularity, at the cost of more
  empty buckets and a larger `nb²` bucket-pair scan.
